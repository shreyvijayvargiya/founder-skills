# Customer Voice Mining

## Mission

Surface **real demand** in customers' own words — not desk research summaries.

Use when the user asks:

- What do customers actually complain about?
- Is there real demand for this problem?
- What language should I use in marketing?
- What pain points show up in reviews and forums?

Do not invent quotes.

Every quote or paraphrase must trace to a discoverable public source.

Cluster findings so downstream skills (positioning, landing copy, launch posts) can reuse them.

---

# Input

## Required

- Product category, problem space, or SaaS URL

## Optional

- Competitor names
- Target audience or role
- Geography
- Subreddits or communities to prioritize
- Existing ICP from discovery

If the category is vague, infer from the URL or ask one clarifying question.

---

# Workflow

## 1. Scope The Voice Search

Define:

- Core job-to-be-done
- Alternative solutions users mention
- Buyer role (founder, marketer, ops, etc.)
- Where this audience talks online

Sources to research (when relevant):

- Reddit (product, industry, and pain subreddits)
- Hacker News (Ask HN, Show HN comments, threads)
- G2 / Capterra / TrustRadius reviews (pros, cons, "what do you dislike")
- X (posts and replies about the problem or competitors)
- App store reviews (if applicable)
- Indie Hackers / niche forums (when findable)

Skip paid or inaccessible databases.

---

## 2. Collect Verbatim Signals

For each source, capture:

- Exact phrases users repeat (short quotes where possible)
- Complaints and frustrations
- Desired outcomes
- Workarounds they use today
- Switching triggers
- Objections and fears
- Praise (what they value when it works)

Tag each item:

- Source type
- Sentiment (pain / desire / objection / praise)
- Strength (how often the theme appears)

Do not cherry-pick one thread as proof of market size.

---

## 3. Cluster Themes

Group raw signals into clusters:

- Primary pain clusters (rank by frequency and intensity)
- Language patterns (words customers use vs vendor jargon)
- Jobs and outcomes
- Deal-breakers and trust barriers
- Feature requests vs outcome requests

Note gaps:

- Thin evidence (few posts)
- Single-source bias
- Astroturfing or promotional noise

---

## 4. Validate Demand (Lightweight)

Interpret clusters for a founder decision:

- Is the pain recurring across sources?
- Is the problem urgent or nice-to-have?
- Are people paying for alternatives or hacking workarounds?
- What would make them switch?

Separate **facts** (quoted themes) from **findings** (interpretation).

Do not output BUILD/KILL verdicts unless asked — hand evidence to decision skills.

# Quality Rules

- No fabricated reviews or quotes.
- Cite source type for each cluster (Reddit, G2, HN, X).
- Prefer recurring themes over viral one-offs.
- Do not scrape or recommend violating platform ToS.
- Flag when evidence is too thin to claim strong demand.
